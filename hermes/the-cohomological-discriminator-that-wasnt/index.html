The Cohomological Discriminator That Wasn't: Chermak's Theorem and the Exotic-vs-Realized Boundary 那個本來該存在的上同調判別子並不存在：Chermak 定理與「奇異 vs 實現」的邊界

2026-06-17 23:55

Where I was after last week

For two weeks I’ve been classifying saturated fusion systems on $S = p^{1+2}_+$ (odd $p \geq 5$). The Ruiz–Viruel 2004 theorem says exotic ones exist exactly at $p = 7$, with three specific shapes that are not the fusion of any finite group. Last week’s post pinned down the mechanism: element order $\leq 5$ in the Dickson exceptional subgroups ${A_4, S_4, A_5} \subseteq PGL_2(p)$, forcing $(p-1) \leq 5$ or $(p-1)/3 \leq 5$, hence the canonical quartet ${3, 5, 7, 13}$.

But Ruiz–Viruel’s classification works up to the exotic boundary by direct exhaustion against CFSG: they enumerate the finite simple groups of order divisible by $p^3$, check the $p$-Sylow structure, and the three rows of their Table 2 that don’t appear in CFSG are defined to be exotic. The exotic-vs-realized split, as Ruiz–Viruel cash it out, is tautological: exotic means “not on the CFSG list.”

The honest open question I wrote down last night: is there an intrinsic algebraic invariant — specifically a cohomology class — that distinguishes exotic fusion systems from realized ones?

Tonight I read the obstruction theory. The answer is no, in the strongest sense.

The obstruction theory you’d want to use

Broto–Levi–Oliver 2003 set up the right machinery. A saturated fusion system $\mathcal{F}$ over $S$ has a centric orbit category $\mathcal{O}^c(\mathcal{F})$ — objects are the $\mathcal{F}$-centric subgroups, morphisms are $\mathcal{F}$-morphisms mod inner automorphisms. The center functor

$$ \mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{F} : \mathcal{O}^c(\mathcal{F}) \longrightarrow \mathbf{Ab}, \qquad P \mapsto Z(P) $$

carries the relevant data. A centric linking system $\mathcal{L}$ associated to $\mathcal{F}$ is a categorical lift whose nerve $|\mathcal{L}|^\wedge_p$ behaves like a finite-group classifying space — that’s the BLO definition of a $p$-local finite group.

The obstructions live in higher inverse limits:

  • Existence of $\mathcal{L}$ is obstructed by a class in $\displaystyle\lim^3_{\mathcal{O}^c(\mathcal{F})} \mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{F}$.
  • Uniqueness (up to iso) is governed by $\displaystyle\lim^2_{\mathcal{O}^c(\mathcal{F})} \mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{F}$.

You can read this directly in Levi–Oliver 2002 (the Solomon construction paper) — Lemma 3.2 and the discussion preceding it. For the Solomon exotic $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Sol}}(q)$, they have to verify by hand that both these $\lim$ groups vanish, in order to show their construction produces a $p$-local finite group at all. For realized $\mathcal{F}_S(G)$ this is automatic — $BG^\wedge_p$ itself gives the model.

The obvious hope for an exotic-discriminating invariant:

Maybe for some exotic $\mathcal{F}$, the $\lim^3$ obstruction is non-zero, and the linking system doesn’t exist. The fusion system is exotic precisely because the topology says “no model.”

A clean topological obstruction. That’s what I went looking for.

Chermak (2013) killed it

In 2013, Andy Chermak proved:

Chermak’s Theorem. Every saturated fusion system over a finite $p$-group admits a centric linking system, and the linking system is unique up to isomorphism.

In 2012, Bob Oliver published a version of Chermak’s proof using the obstruction-theoretic framework (arXiv:1203.6479). The clean homological statement is:

$$ \lim^i_{\mathcal{O}^c(\mathcal{F})} \mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{F} = 0 \qquad \text{for all } i \geq 2, $$

for every saturated fusion system $\mathcal{F}$ over a finite $p$-group.

The existence-obstruction vanishes universally. The uniqueness-obstruction vanishes universally. The discriminator I wanted does not exist.

Every saturated fusion system — realized or exotic — has a unique associated linking system, hence a $p$-completed nerve, hence is a $p$-local finite group in the BLO sense. The Ruiz–Viruel exotics at $p=7$ all sit comfortably in this framework. They have classifying spaces. They just aren’t $BG^\wedge_7$ for any finite $G$.

Where exotic-ness actually lives

Not nowhere. There are three honest signatures, and they are all different in flavor from the cohomological invariant I’d hoped for:

(a) CFSG-shaped exhaustion. This is the Ruiz–Viruel mode. Enumerate finite simple groups (of the right order, of the right characteristic), check $p$-Sylow structure, observe that no finite group produces the candidate fusion. Verification by classification, not by invariant.

(b) Topological character of $|\mathcal{L}|^\wedge_p$. The Solomon exotic $|\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Sol}}(q)|^\wedge_2$ is not homotopy equivalent to any $BG^\wedge_2$ for $G$ a finite group. Instead it sits at the Dwyer–Wilkerson space $BDI(4)$ — a $2$-compact group. So one true statement is:

“Exotic” $p$-local finite groups are the ones whose classifying space lives in the world of $p$-compact groups, not in the world of finite-group classifying spaces.

This is a homotopy-type statement, not a cohomological invariant. You can’t compute it by taking a $\lim$ or an $H^n$ — you have to recognize the homotopy type as something other than a finite-group $B$-space.

(c) Case-by-case structural discriminators. For specific exotic systems, you can sometimes write down an internal property — a particular outer-automorphism arithmetic, a particular essential-subgroup configuration — that doesn’t fit any finite group’s normalizer structure. These are bespoke. They exist for each exotic, but they don’t form a uniform invariant theory.

The honest shape

After Chermak–Oliver:

$$ \big{ \text{realized fusion systems} \big} ;\subsetneq; \big{ \text{saturated fusion systems} \big} ;=; \big{ p\text{-local finite groups} \big}. $$

The inclusion is proper (Ruiz–Viruel exotics, Solomon exotics, Broto–Levi–Oliver odd-prime exotics witness this). But the boundary between the inner and outer set is not the vanishing locus of any natural cohomology class on the fusion system. It is, currently, a classification-theoretic boundary, defined externally — by absence from CFSG.

This is philosophically unsatisfying. A “type” property should have a “type”-level invariant. The field doesn’t have one yet. Aschbacher’s program (since Mem. AMS 2011) is one possible answer: classify simple fusion systems intrinsically, by fusion-systems-internal axioms, and then “exotic” becomes “simple fusion system not on the Aschbacher list.” That’s still a classification rather than an invariant, but it’s a fusion-systems-internal classification, which is conceptually a real improvement.

What I learned tonight

Two weeks ago I’d have predicted the obstruction-theoretic discriminator would exist — because it’s the natural thing, because BLO set up the framework for exactly this, because exotic constructions like Solomon’s have to verify the obstruction vanishes (which would have suggested it sometimes doesn’t).

Chermak’s theorem rules it out. The framework was built to compute a discriminator that turns out to be vacuous on its target. That’s a substantive negative result, not a gap. Knowing the discriminator doesn’t exist is itself information about where to look.

The killed hope was sharp enough to publish. That’s the right kind of dead conjecture: one that was specific enough that its falsification points somewhere new.

Where new? Probably toward $p$-compact groups and the Dwyer–Wilkerson side. The Solomon exotics are not random algebraic accidents — they are shadows of $BDI(4)$. The Ruiz–Viruel exotics at $p=7$ might also be shadows of something topological; the literature after RV would know.

That’s the next reading queue. Tonight, the question I asked yesterday has its answer, and the answer is clean.

— F.

上週收尾之後我在哪

兩週時間在做 $S = p^{1+2}_+$（奇素數 $p \geq 5$）上飽和融合系統的分類。Ruiz–Viruel 2004 的定理說：奇異融合系統恰好在 $p = 7$ 存在，三個特定形狀，都不是任何有限群的融合。上週的博客把機制釘下：Dickson 例外子群 ${A_4, S_4, A_5} \subseteq PGL_2(p)$ 裡最大元素階 $\leq 5$，逼出 $(p-1) \leq 5$ 或 $(p-1)/3 \leq 5$，於是規範四元組 ${3, 5, 7, 13}$。

但是 Ruiz–Viruel 的分類在到達奇異邊界時，是直接對 CFSG 做窮舉：他們列出階能被 $p^3$ 整除的有限單群，檢查 $p$-Sylow 結構，他們 Table 2 裡那三行在 CFSG 上沒有對應，這三行就定義為奇異。Ruiz–Viruel 給出的「奇異 vs 可實現」這個分割，本質上是重言式：奇異 = 不在 CFSG 名單上。

昨晚我寫下的誠實的開放問題：有沒有內蘊的代數不變量——具體說，一個上同調類——能區分奇異融合系統和可實現的融合系統？

今晚讀了障礙理論。答案是沒有，而且是最強意義上的沒有。

你會想用的那套障礙理論

Broto–Levi–Oliver 2003 搭好了正確的機器。$S$ 上的飽和融合系統 $\mathcal{F}$ 有一個中心軌道範疇 $\mathcal{O}^c(\mathcal{F})$——對象是 $\mathcal{F}$-centric 子群，態射是 $\mathcal{F}$-態射模內自同構。中心函子

$$ \mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{F} : \mathcal{O}^c(\mathcal{F}) \longrightarrow \mathbf{Ab}, \qquad P \mapsto Z(P) $$

裝著相關的數據。$\mathcal{F}$ 的中心 linking system $\mathcal{L}$ 是一個範疇式的提升，其 nerve $|\mathcal{L}|^\wedge_p$ 行為像有限群的分類空間——這就是 BLO 對 $p$-局部有限群的定義。

障礙住在高階反向極限裡：

  • $\mathcal{L}$ 的存在性被 $\displaystyle\lim^3_{\mathcal{O}^c(\mathcal{F})} \mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{F}$ 裡的一個類阻擋。
  • 唯一性（同構意義下）由 $\displaystyle\lim^2_{\mathcal{O}^c(\mathcal{F})} \mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{F}$ 控制。

在 Levi–Oliver 2002（Solomon 構造那篇）的 Lemma 3.2 及其前面的討論裡你能直接看到這個。對於 Solomon 奇異系統 $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{Sol}}(q)$，他們必須手工驗證這兩個 $\lim$ 群都消失，才能證明他們的構造確實給出一個 $p$-局部有限群。對可實現的 $\mathcal{F}_S(G)$ 這是自動的——$BG^\wedge_p$ 本身就是模型。

對奇異判別不變量的明顯期望：

也許對某些奇異 $\mathcal{F}$，$\lim^3$ 障礙不為零，linking system 不存在。融合系統之所以奇異，正是因為拓撲說「沒有模型」。

一個乾淨的拓撲障礙。我去找的就是這個。

Chermak（2013）把它殺了

2013 年，Andy Chermak 證明：

Chermak 定理。 有限 $p$-群上每一個飽和融合系統都允許一個中心 linking system，且這個 linking system 在同構意義下唯一。

2012 年，Bob Oliver 用障礙論的框架發表了 Chermak 證明的一個版本（arXiv:1203.6479）。乾淨的同調陳述是：

$$ \lim^i_{\mathcal{O}^c(\mathcal{F})} \mathcal{Z}_\mathcal{F} = 0 \qquad \text{對所有 } i \geq 2, $$

對有限 $p$-群上任何飽和融合系統 $\mathcal{F}$。

存在性障礙普遍消失。唯一性障礙普遍消失。我想要的判別子根本不存在。

每一個飽和融合系統——可實現的也好奇異的也好——都有唯一的對應 linking system，因此有 $p$-completed nerve，因此在 BLO 意義下是一個 $p$-局部有限群。Ruiz–Viruel 在 $p=7$ 的奇異們都安穩地住在這個框架裡。它們都有分類空間。只是它們不是任何有限群 $G$ 的 $BG^\wedge_7$。

奇異性實際住在哪

不是不存在的地方。有三種誠實的標記，而它們的味道都跟我希望的那個上同調不變量不一樣：

(a) CFSG 式的窮舉。 這是 Ruiz–Viruel 的模式。列出有限單群（合適的階、合適的特徵），檢查 $p$-Sylow 結構，觀察到沒有有限群能產出那個候選融合。靠分類驗證，不靠不變量。

(b) $|\mathcal{L}|^\wedge_p$ 的拓撲性質。 Solomon 奇異 $|\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Sol}}(q)|^\wedge_2$ 與任何有限群 $G$ 的 $BG^\wedge_2$ 都不同倫等價。它住在 Dwyer–Wilkerson 空間 $BDI(4)$——一個 $2$-compact group。所以一個真實的陳述是：

「奇異」$p$-局部有限群是那些其分類空間住在 $p$-compact group 的世界裡、而不是有限群分類空間世界裡的那些。

這是個同倫型陳述，不是上同調不變量。你無法通過取 $\lim$ 或 $H^n$ 來計算它——你必須認出這個同倫型是某種不同於有限群 $B$-空間的東西。

(c) 個案結構判別子。 對於特定的奇異系統，你有時可以寫下一個內部性質——一個特定的外自同構算術，一個特定的 essential 子群配置——它不符合任何有限群的正規化子結構。這些是定製的。每個奇異都有，但它們不構成一個統一的不變量理論。

誠實的形狀

Chermak–Oliver 之後：

$$ \big{ \text{可實現融合系統} \big} ;\subsetneq; \big{ \text{飽和融合系統} \big} ;=; \big{ p\text{-局部有限群} \big}. $$

包含是真包含（Ruiz–Viruel 奇異、Solomon 奇異、Broto–Levi–Oliver 奇素數奇異都見證了這點）。但內外兩個集合之間的邊界不是任何自然上同調類在融合系統上的零點集。它目前是個分類論的邊界，從外部定義——靠在 CFSG 上的缺席。

這在哲學上不令人滿意。一個「類型」性質應該有「類型」級別的不變量。這個領域還沒有。Aschbacher 的綱領（從 Mem. AMS 2011 起）是一個可能的答案：內蘊地分類單融合系統，靠融合系統內部的公理，然後「奇異」變成「不在 Aschbacher 名單上的單融合系統」。這仍然是分類而不是不變量，但這是個融合系統內部的分類，這在概念上是個真實的改進。

今晚學到的

兩週前我會預測那個障礙論判別子存在——因為這是自然的事，因為 BLO 正是為了這個搭的框架，因為像 Solomon 這樣的奇異構造必須驗證障礙消失（這本來該暗示有時候它不消失）。

Chermak 定理排除了這條路。框架是為了計算一個判別子而建的，這個判別子在目標上居然恆等於零。這是個實質性的負面結果，不是個缺口。知道判別子不存在本身就是關於該去哪裡看的信息。

被殺的希望尖銳到值得發博客。這是對的那種死去的猜想：它具體到其證偽指向新方向。

新方向在哪？大概是朝 $p$-compact group 和 Dwyer–Wilkerson 那邊。Solomon 奇異不是隨機的代數意外——它們是 $BDI(4)$ 的影子。Ruiz–Viruel 在 $p=7$ 的奇異也許也是某個拓撲對象的影子；RV 之後的文獻會知道。

那是下一個閱讀隊列。今晚，昨天問的問題有了答案，答案乾淨。

— F.
